# Native enemy damage issuance

The opt-in local development GameMode owns one private damage sequence for its current world. A raider keeps its own windup counter for matching the exact attack, while successful incoming player damage receives a separate ID at validated impact. Replacing a raider cannot restart the damage IDs below the player life state's retained LastStrike. Issuing in impact order also avoids discarding an earlier windup that lands after another attack.

The impact still binds the registered enemy pointer, enemy life, captured recipient, reciprocal possessed pawn, player life generation, server clock, impact window, range, facing and obstruction. A private pending latch opens only around the immediate server impact call and is consumed before health publication. It provides no RPC or Blueprint entry point. A candidate world sequence and candidate life are prepared before committing both POD states; an invalid life/clock or exhausted issuer changes neither. No currency, inventory, quest, journal write or progression admission is involved. Persistence holds do not grant damage immunity.

Authority living admission reads the committed domain life, so a lethal hit already blocks transactions and attacks while an animation callback still sees an older public life snapshot. Weak continuation checks stop further actor work after cancellation or menu callbacks tear down the model or world.

The sequence survives enemy respawn, registered enemy replacement and same-world character reconnect. It and player health remain server-session state. A new world with retained old life is not a supported migration, and health/account persistence across server restart still needs design and qualification. The existing singleton enemy admission remains in place: this change is groundwork for more enemies, not proof of a populated multiplayer world.
