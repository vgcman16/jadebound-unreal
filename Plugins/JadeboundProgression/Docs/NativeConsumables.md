# Native healing consumables

The opt-in development combat slice uses the existing catalog Balm (item 7): three jade coins to buy, one to sell, maximum stack ten. One owned dose restores up to 25 of the server's 100 health. Full health, death, unavailable ownership or a held inventory write preserve the item. Ordinary standalone balm retains its existing local hero and hotbar path. Development mode remains disabled by default and unavailable in Shipping.

The owner submits slot, item, inventory revision and the life generation captured when its pack control was rendered. No client health, target, effect amount or time is accepted. The authority revalidates its registered model, controller, possessed body, world, pack context, writer readiness, exact inventory intent and current life. Buying uses the existing authoritative catalog, cost, capacity, revision and merchant-distance checks. Native pack tiles support inspection and use; legacy hotbar dragging remains scoped to its existing standalone context.

`Transactions/NativeBalmRules.h` prepares a complete life candidate before calling the actual inventory core's consume operation. Success advances inventory once and publishes life through nonthrowing assignments. Generation, death fields and accepted-strike history remain intact. The actor freezes the exact inventory journal candidate before presentation or acknowledgment callbacks. All ordinary progression admission refuses that pending image, including reentry during a writer callback.

A failed journal open, write, flush or readback does not roll back consumption or replay healing. The held inventory candidate is retried as data. Incoming native damage and manual revive use their separate life gate and continue during the hold. Publication reads the current retained life, so damage during the writer callback and later death/revive cannot be overwritten by an earlier healed candidate. The `Use` action receipt reports the consumed and verified inventory revisions plus held status; it does not fabricate client success before acknowledgment.

The journal persists inventory, equipment, currency and quest progress. This increment does **not** persist health or life generation across server restart. Same-session retained health and durable consumption have separate lifetimes. Production identity, restart recovery, independent-client transport, rendered controls and packaged gameplay require their own qualification.

The portable suite links the actual `ProgressionCore.cpp` and exercises fixed recovery, clamping, full/dead/invalid/stale refusals, exact replay tokens, revision boundaries, isolation and allocation failure before consumption. Its allocation override is confined to that executable and never linked into Unreal. The native fixture uses real isolated journals, real native strikes and measured world frames. Its owner/registry/menu assembly and controlled retry deadline are trusted fixture setup; direct RPC implementation calls are handler tests, not network transport. See the publication validation document for executed results.
